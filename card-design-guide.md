# Into the Wild — Card Design Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the design principles used when creating new cards for *Into the Wild*.

It is intended for expansion authors and future contributors.

Gameplay rules are defined in **core-game-engine.md**.

Card data is defined in **card-specification.md**.

---

# Design Philosophy

Every card should represent a real ecological relationship.

Cards are designed around organisms, resources, habitats, and natural processes found within the expansion's geographic region.

Gameplay should emerge from ecological interactions rather than abstract fantasy mechanics.

---

# Card Categories

Every card belongs to one of three card types.

## Energy

Energy Cards represent ecological resources required to sustain life.

They are permanent cards that generate one Energy type.

Energy types are:

* Water
* Flora
* Fauna

Energy cards represent resources.

Examples include:

* River
* Seeds
* Acorns
* Prickly Pear Fruit
* Mesquite Beans
* Ground Squirrel
* Rabbit

---

## Habitat

Habitat Cards represent organisms that become permanent members of a player's ecosystem.

Habitat Cards are divided into 2 kingdoms Habitat flora and Habitat fauna.

### Habitat Fauna 

- Herbivore

Herbivore Habitat Cards represent organisms whose primary food source is plant biomass.

Examples:

- Mule Deer
- Desert Cottontail
- Gambel's Quail

---

- Omnivore

Omnivore Habitat Cards represent organisms that consume both plant and animal biomass.

Examples:

- Black Bear
- Common Raven
- Striped Skunk

---

- Carnivore

Carnivore Habitat Cards represent organisms whose primary food source is animal biomass.

Examples:

- Bobcat
- Mountain Lion
- Coyote

### Habitat flora

These may include trees, cacti, vines, and other non-food plants 
---

## Event

Event Cards represent temporary ecological effects.

Events resolve immediately before being placed into the Compost pile.

Examples include:

* Weather
* Catastrophic Events
* Invasive Species
* Human Impacts

---

# Rank Guidelines

Rank represents an events, organism's, and enery ecological significance within the game.

Higher Rank generally indicates greater ecological impact, increased resource requirements, and stronger gameplay effects.

Ranks should increase gradually throughout an expansion.


---

## Low Rank

Typical characteristics:

* Common species
* Small organisms
* Few abilities
* Low Energy Cost

---

## Medium Rank

Typical characteristics:

* Larger organisms
* More specialized diets
* Moderate abilities
* Moderate Energy Cost

---

## High Rank

Typical characteristics:

* Large organisms
* Complex behaviors
* Powerful abilities
* High Energy Cost

---

# Energy Costs

Energy Costs should reflect the ecological requirements of the organism or habitat being represented.

General principles:

* Most living organisms require Water.
* Herbivores primarily consume Flora.
* Carnivores primarily consume Fauna.
* Omnivores consume both Flora and Fauna.

Higher-ranked organisms should generally require greater total Energy, reflecting their greater ecological demands.


---

# Ability Design

Abilities should represent observable ecological behaviors.

Examples include:

* Pollination
* Seed dispersal
* Camouflage
* Migration
* Burrowing
* Pack hunting
* Scavenging
* Drought tolerance

Avoid abstract or magical abilities.

---

# Educational Content

Facts should be:

* Scientifically accurate.
* Easy to understand.
* Independent of gameplay.

Facts should educate without explaining game mechanics.

---

# Artwork

Artwork should accurately represent the subject depicted on the card.

Illustrations should prioritize:

* Correct anatomy for organisms.
* Correct coloration when color is used.
* Characteristic behavior when depicting organisms.
* Recognizable habitat or environmental setting.
* Scientifically recognizable features.

Artistic style may vary, but scientific accuracy should remain a priority.

---

# Expansion Design

Each expansion represents a specific geographic region or ecosystem.

Examples may include:

- A national forest
- A ranger district
- A watershed
- A mountain range
- A desert
- A grassland
- A wetland
- An island ecosystem

Cards within an expansion should naturally occur within the geographic region or ecosystem represented by that expansion.

Organisms, habitats, resources, and events should be ecologically consistent with one another.

---

# Balance Principles

When designing new cards:

* Rank should reflect ecological significance.
* Energy Cost should reflect ecological requirements.
* Abilities should be thematic rather than arbitrary.
* No single card should dominate gameplay without meaningful cost, counterplay, or opportunity cost.

---

# Consistency

When multiple cards represent the same species:

* The organism should remain scientifically accurate.
* Facts should not contradict previous printings.
* Artwork may change between expansions.
* Mechanics may vary to represent seasonal behavior, life stage, habitat, or ecological role.

---

# Design Goals

Every new card should satisfy the following goals:

* Represent a real organism, resource, habitat, or ecological process.
* Be scientifically grounded.
* Be mechanically clear.
* Fit naturally within its expansion.
* Contribute meaningful gameplay decisions.
* Teach players something about the natural world.
